Your return will be lower if a redemption fee is applied to your account. After successive quarterly gains to start the year, small/mid cap equities posted a modest loss for the third quarter. The Cambiar SMID Fund was unable to sidestep the weaker environment, registering a relatively in-line return with the Russell 2500 Value Index. The portfolio benefited from positive stock selection in Financials and Energy, while below-benchmark performance within Healthcare and Industrials weighed on 3Q results. Buy/sell activity was more elevated in comparison to past quarters, consisting of four initiations, six liquidations, and incremental adds/trims to existing positions. New purchases spanned various sectors, and our underwriting process remains focused on identifying company-specific catalysts that can drive earnings/ multiple expansion, vs. trying to fit a specific market narrative. Examples include Hasbro (leading game/toy manufacturer that is ramping into the digital gaming space) and Gartner (tech-focused research and advisory services). In contrast to the broad-based positive returns in 2Q, sector performance was more mixed in the third quarter. The sharp rise in oil/diesel prices was a notable tailwind for the Energy sector but was a drag on the transport- heavy Industrials sector. The resulting smaller wallet share for consumers due to higher prices at the pump was also a negative for Consumer Discretionary stocks. Meanwhile, bond-proxy sectors such as Utilities and Real Estate posted weaker returns in response to higher bond yields, while Healthcare stocks closed higher in the quarter. Finally, the all-important Technology sector was essentially flat for 3Q, which we would consider a win – given the parabolic move in tech stocks over the first half of the year. The quarter was a good example of the reduced operational/financial flexibility that smaller cap stocks possess (vs. large caps) in dealing with macro variables such as rates and energy prices. The wide move in energy prices has been a prominent headline in 2026. Given the potential for oil prices to contract upon resolution of the Middle East conflict, we chose to have limited exposure in the Energy sector with a single position in HF Sinclair – which operates branded gas stations and also has a refining segment. The blowout in crack spreads (measures how much refiners earn from turning oil into diesel and gasoline) has resulted in windfall profits for Sinclair in recent quarters, with the stock more than doubling in 2026. Given the reduced upside, we sold the position in favor of Expand Energy, which is the largest natural gas producer in the U.S. Natural gas prices are currently low due to expectations for a warmer winter (El Nino), but should recover over a longer arc due to stronger demand trends (e.g., the ongoing datacenter buildout). Expand hedges a portion of its production, providing some level of revenue visibility. The company’s recent acquisition of Twin Eagle (natural gas storage/pipeline assets) should provide additional synergies and free cashflow upside. The outsized move in diesel prices during the quarter contributed to weaker returns in Southwest Airlines and SAIA Inc. – two of the portfolio’s holdings in the Industrials sector. SAIA is a shorter-haul transport company (trucking); the company can offset higher diesel prices via fuel surcharges, and reduced competition due to the more challenging environment will ultimately be a positive for the industry and SAIA. Consistent with our focus on low leverage, SAIA’s net cash balance sheet provides the company with good optionality as it relates to enhancing shareholder returns. On a more positive note, the portfolio incurred a takeout during the quarter, as ACV Auctions (wholesale vehicle auction marketplace) was acquired by Copart, a larger player in the industry. Although the all-cash offer resulted in a meaningful gain in the stock during the quarter, it is our view that Copart is getting a very good deal vs. our fair value estimate for ACVA. In light of the expanded market position and added technology capabilities that this transaction now provides Copart, we initiated a position in the company. Cambiar’s overweight exposure to Technology added value from an allocation perspective but was overshadowed by below-benchmark performance for the period. At a stock level, ON Semiconductor and Lattice Semi each registered declines in the July rotation out of hardware names. Given the strong gains in both stocks during the first half of the year, perhaps some giveback was warranted in 3Q, and we remain constructive on the multi-year earnings outlook for both positions. The recent purchase of Nutanix (NTNX) is off to a good start, with the stock moving higher in response to a positive earnings report. Nutanix utilization software allows servers to run multiple independent operating systems simultaneously, maximizing server efficiency. With the anticipated growth in on-premises hardware (see Dell), NTNX should be in excellent position to grow revenues/earnings.
