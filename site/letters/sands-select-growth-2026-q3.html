September 30, 2026 Sands Capital Select Growth Quarterly Report (USD) 8 The top individual absolute detractors were Carpenter Technology, Lam Research, Micron Technology, Seagate Technology, and Sphere Entertainment. Carpenter Technology is a leading supplier of specialty nickel alloys for the aerospace industry. Shares declined amid concerns that higher oil prices could weigh on aerospace aftermarket demand. Fiscal fourth-quarter results also fell short of expectations in some areas, with the business facing a high bar following strong recent operating results and share price performance during the first half of 2026. More specifically, operating profit came in modestly below expectations, primarily due to the timing of corporate expenses, while management issued a conservative fiscal 2027 outlook. However, margins in Carpenter’s Specialty Alloy Operations business expanded 700 basis points year over year, consistent with our expectations that recovering aerospace production and tightening metals supply will support continued pricing power. With our investment thesis unchanged, we believe management’s fiscal 2027 guidance is conservative relative to the underlying earnings opportunity. The drawdown in Lam Research, Micron Technology, and Seagate Technology reflects a reassessment of the industry’s growth trajectory rather than a deterioration in demand, in our view. Following 12 months of significant upward earnings revisions, we expect the pace of further upgrades to moderate as pricing gains slow and shipment volumes become a more important driver of revenue growth. Long-term supply agreements should provide memory suppliers with greater demand visibility, although they may also limit some additional pricing and margin upside. We are also monitoring the potential for additional supply from Chinese memory producer ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT). CXMT is a credible competitor in conventional dynamic random-access memory (DRAM), and our forecasts account for its continued growth. However, high-bandwidth memory (HBM) is more complex to manufacture and requires substantially more wafer capacity per bit. We estimate that CXMT remains two to three years behind industry leaders in HBM and therefore do not expect its expansion to resolve supply constraints in that market in the near term. Moreover, as leading suppliers devote more capacity to HBM, they constrain the capacity available for conventional DRAM. Our holdings provide exposure to different parts of this value chain. Micron supplies HBM and other memory products, Seagate serves hard-disk storage demand, and Lam Research supplies equipment used in increasingly complex memory manufacturing processes. While the outlook for each business differs, we believe their current valuations collectively discount a near-term industry downturn that is inconsistent with our expectations for sustained AI-related demand and earnings growth. Sphere Entertainment is creating a new category of immersive live entertainment. Shares came under pressure during the quarter amid signs of weaker Las Vegas tourism and concerns that growth from "The Wizard of Oz" could moderate against tougher year-ago comparisons
