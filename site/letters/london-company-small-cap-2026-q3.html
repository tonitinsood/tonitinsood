Small Cap Sector Influence We are bottom-up stock pickers, but sector exposures influenced relative performance as follows: ● What Helped: Underweight Utilities (a weaker performing sector) ● What Hurt: Underweight Health Care (a better performing sector) Trades During the Quarter ● Exited: UniFirst Corporation (UNF) – Sold following the announced acquisition by Cintas (CTAS). The stock was trading near the deal price. In addition, because CTAS' market cap is well outside our small-cap investable universe, we chose to exit the position before closing rather than receive CTAS shares. ● Initiated: Churchill Downs Incorporated (CHDN) – CHDN is a business we know well from other portfolios. CHDN owns a unique collection of racing, gaming, and entertainment businesses, anchored by the Kentucky Derby, one of the most iconic events in sports. Its regional casinos and historical racing venues generate steady, recurring cash flow in markets where regulation limits new competition. Management has been an excellent steward of capital, investing in high-return projects, buying back shares opportunistically, and exiting businesses that fall short of its standards, an approach that has driven strong cash flow growth. Recent share price weakness created an attractive entry point, as the market appears focused on near-term uncertainties while overlooking its lasting strengths. We believe this combination of irreplaceable assets, durable cash generation, and disciplined leadership, at today's more attractive valuation, fits our Quality-at-a-Reasonable- Price approach and positions CHDN to compound shareholder value over the long term. ● Increased: McGrath RentCorp (MGRC) – Added to the name to complete a full 3% position, reflecting our confidence in the long-term thesis. ● Exited: Enovis Corporation (ENOV) – Exited our position reflecting a loss of confidence in management following a recently announced acquisition. The position was relatively small, and we didn't have conviction to add to it. ● Increased: Vontier Corp (VNT) – Addition reflects improving operating results and what we believe remains an attractive valuation, with healthy demand across most of the business, cost savings progressing well, and continued share repurchases. Looking Ahead The economic backdrop remains supportive in many respects. Growth is solid, manufacturing and services activity are improving, and corporate earnings continue to exceed elevated expectations. At the same time, the headwinds have grown more pronounced. The Fed has begun tightening, energy prices and Treasury yields are elevated, and higher borrowing and living costs are weighing on consumers, particularly at the lower end of the income spectrum. The AI investment cycle remains a powerful engine of growth. Yet, its scale invites comparisons to past infrastructure booms, where investors who were right about the buildout still endured significant drawdowns along the way. Early signs of stress have also emerged in the lowest tiers of credit. While we do not attempt to predict the path of rates or the economy, we believe these crosscurrents argue for discipline.
