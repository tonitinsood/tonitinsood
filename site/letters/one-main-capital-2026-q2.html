Philadelphia Semiconductor Index

long

The SPX and RTY had their best quarter since 2020, as the Iran conflict stalled (at least temporarily) and oil once again began flowing through the Strait of Hormuz. The strong performance was largely driven by AI infrastructure and bottleneck beneficiaries, who are seeing strong datacenter capex and robust demand for memory chips. Accordingly, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) gained 88% in the period, its best quarterly performance since its 1994 inception. Unfortunately for 1MC, we had no direct exposure to this AI theme. The commodity AI beneficiaries, such as memory chip manufacturers, lack the quality and durability characteristics I typically look for in core holdings, while the differentiated and specialized AI beneficiaries have been selling for multiples that I am uncomfortable paying, especially for rapidly evolving technologies. While the memory bottleneck theme fit the characteristics I seek in catalyst-driven opportunistic positions — asymmetrically cheap on book value with a very strong pricing upcycle — I failed to identify it before the parabolic moves . At this point, these bets are significantly less attractive, as they require strong conviction on the duration of the current commodity pricing cycle while economic theory practically guarantees that capital will chase returns right into a glut. Some public tech investors that I have great respect for, and know the space much better than me, have suggested this cycle may run longer than typical technology booms due to the discipline shown by the key player s involved. However, I am not eager to bet our capital on the idea that this time is in fact different. Despite what appears to be a speculative fever in some pockets of the AI trade, I remain optimistic that the technology will indeed drive sufficient long-term productivity gains to justify sustained infrastructure investment and support strong real GDP growth in the years ahead. This would bode well for our portfolio. In markets showing increasing signs of such momentum, it is tempting to chase the hot thing of the day. Rest assured, 1MC will maintain its longstanding strategy: ignoring the noise, not chasing the crowd, and staying true to our mission of investing in good businesses trading at reasonable multiples of cash flow, and supplementing our core holdings with a few high-convexity, catalyst-driven opportunistic positions when available. I remain excited about the business prospects across the Fund's holdings, as well as upcoming catalysts for our opportunistic positions, and look forward to seeing what the future holds.

Hilton Grand Vacations

long

Current Positioning At quarter -end, the Fund’s top five positions were Basic-Fit (BFIT NA), Hilton Grand Vacations (HGV), International Workplace Group (IWG), KKR & Co (KKR), and MasterCraft (MCFT). Together, these accounted for approximately 65% of capital. LMB sits just outside of the top five and represents a high- single-digit percentage of capital. Hilton Grand Vacations (HGV) Earlier this year, the Fund initiated a position in Hilton Grand Vacations (HGV), a global timeshare company that develops, markets, sells, manages, and operates timeshare resorts primarily under the Hilton Grand Vacations brand. HGV spun out of Hilton Wor ldwide in 2017 and has since acquired several other operators, growing to more than 200 properties and over 700,000 Club members. The company’s vacation ownership interests (VOIs) allow customers to advance-purchase a lifetime of stays — either at a specific location, or, through a points -based platform, at any resort of their choosing — with a median purchase price of around $30k. VOIs allow buyers to split the total cost of ownership of a vacation property with other owners. Most HGV resorts offer amenities well-suited for families–multiple rooms, kitchens, washers/dryers–making them more attractive than standard hotel rooms. Furthermore, the points-based platform offers flexibility by adapting to members’ changing vacation needs over time. While cyclical, HGV’s new contract sales have grown consistently over time, including through the GFC . Better yet, roughly 60% of the company’s segment adjusted EBITDA comes from capital light and highly recurring sources such as management fees charged to owners for resort operations and from financing. On the financing front, two out of three HGV buyers finance their purchases with 10 -year fixed -rate secured loans at an average rate of roughly 15%. These loans are packaged and sold as non-recourse ABS at a weighted average cost of 5%, allowing HGV to earn a 10% net interest spread in a highly capital efficient manner. These prime borrowers (average FICO score above 700) are willing to pay subprime rates to borrow small sums ( average balance of just over $20k), as they are effectively buying a monthly payment plan rather than focusing on the sticker price. Because of the ongoing property management fees, financing spread, and likely unit upgrades, approximately 80% of a customer’s lifetime value comes from sales after the initial timeshare purchase. While the timeshare industry carries a somewhat-deserved negative reputation, due to a history of high- pressure and unethical sales tactics, large, branded players like HGV maintain higher standards to comply with brand license agreements. Branded players like HGV also make it easier for members to sell their VOIs back to the company, which then resells them to new members for a profit. The industry’s negative reputation and inaccurate investor perceptions of cyclicality are the primary reasons the shares trade at a single-digit free cash flow multiple.
