Smaller companies have taken the brunt of the pain. Small and micro caps led the market early in 2026. They have since given back significant ground versus the broader market. The Russell 2000 is about 10% below its high and the Russell Microcap about 12%. Over the past month the Russell 2000 has fallen 4% and the Microcap 6%, while the S&P 500 rose about 1.5% and sits within 1–2% of its own high. Both small-cap indexes closed yesterday on October 15 at their lowest levels since the spring. Within that universe, a large number of individual small and microcap stocks are trading at or near 52-week lows, many with no change in their underlying fundamentals, only a change in sentiment and a market that seems not to care. I think this gap is more likely to close than widen from here, most likely through a flat index and outperformance from small and microcap names as buyers look for opportunity. Concentration this extreme has historically been followed by a rotation. After the narrowest stretches on record, equal weight has beaten cap weight by a median of nearly 5 points a year over the following three years. This is the environment our process is built for. Our small-cap skew and focus on under- covered names mean we are buying where the market is least crowded and most mispriced. Our multi-year holding period lets us use this kind of dislocation instead of being forced out by it. I would rather own quality growth businesses at discounted prices before the rotation than chase it after.
