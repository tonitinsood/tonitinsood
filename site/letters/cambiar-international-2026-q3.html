Although overall consumer spending patterns have remained relatively solid, the Discretionary sector has been a weaker segment of the market…our underweight allocation thus far (~4% vs. 9% for the index) has been an additional value-add. Energy and oil prices have been prominent headlines in 2026. We are not in a position to evaluate how the Iran war may conclude. Generally after a ‘right tail’ event such as the conflict in the Middle East, oil prices should contract significantly upon resolution – thus our more limited allocation to the sector. We did increase our exposure during 3Q with the purchase of Petroleo Brasileiro (PBR). We believe PBR offers the widest valuation opportunity in global energy. With a focus on growing production/reserves of low-cost Atlantic Basin crude, PBR runs the best offshore upstream business in the world (again, in our view). Petrobras’ downstream operations are increasingly benefiting from strong refining spreads. We are cognizant of risks in the forms of export taxes in the upstream, price interventions for downstream retail fuel operations, and political arm-twisting the capex budget toward regionally favored projects. Yet we view these considerations to be reflected in PBR’s valuation, resulting in a compelling return profile. The Industrials sector remains a more inconsistent sector for the portfolio, with aggregate returns that trailed the index for the period. While encouraged by the share price recovery in MonotaRO and gains from newer holdings such as MTU Aero Engines and Schneider Electric, these positive return drivers were offset by drawdowns in Daifuku and Grab Holdings. Given a strong run into their 2Q earnings, we viewed the price weakness in Daifuku to be more of a ‘sell the news’ event. Daifuku sells advanced automation equipment for materials handling/warehouses, semiconductor packaging cleanrooms, airport security, and other manufacturing applications. The company’s semi business remains a key growth driver and the 3Q reset in valuations results in a more balanced risk/ reward. Grab Holdings is a tech platform that operates the dominant taxi and food delivery network in Southeast Asia. Local demand is growing low double- digits alongside economic development and mobile connectivity, and Grab has competitive advantages in unique mapping technology and local payments that provides a solid competitive moat vs. new entrants. The YTD decline in the stock has been largely a function of higher fuel prices, as Grab has continued to meet/ exceed key inputs – including revenue projections, EBITDA/FCF, and user growth. The company’s move into financial services has also given investors pause, yet we believe the company can successfully integrate this business into their core rideshare/delivery efforts (Southeast Asia has a large unbanked population). On a more recent note, we were encouraged by the $30 million open market purchase by GRAB CEO Anthony Tan.
