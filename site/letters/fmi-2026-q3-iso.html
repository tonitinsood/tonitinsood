The charts above give a snapshot of the AI At Risk category in the U.S. Relative stock performance is depicted on the left, with a 2-3 standard deviation valuation discount to history on the right. This is not just a U.S. dynamic – the same phenomenon has taken place across developed international markets, as the equivalent charts for Europe below illustrate. Much of this repricing has been a blunt instrument, punishing resilient businesses alongside those truly exposed to AI disruption. Our process has not changed amid this dispersion; we are looking for high-quality, well-financed franchises trading at a discount to intrinsic value. This year’s AI-driven volatility has simply widened the opportunity set. Our portfolios trade at a significant discount to their respective benchmarks on a variety of valuation measures. We think this gap will close over time as the market moves past a blanket AI narrative and reprices these businesses on their own merits. The following three holdings illustrate where we see AI mispricing today. In each case, we have built the position into recent weakness. Jack Henry & Associates Inc. (JKHY) – Small Cap Jack Henry is an enterprise software business serving local commercial banks and credit unions. The company provides core banking software, the back-office technology stack that allows a financial institution to open customer accounts, take deposits, and process transactions. The product’s mission-critical nature is difficult to overstate – without this core, a bank cannot perform its most basic functions. Switching costs define Jack Henry’s competitive advantage, as its risk-averse customers in the highly regulated financial services industry are loath to rip-and-replace essential business infrastructure. Jack Henry has differentiated itself through a highly integrated suite of products and customer service that simplifies operations for the smaller, less-resourced organizations it serves. While Jack Henry doesn’t have the R&D budget available to its much larger competitors, management’s singular focus on this market opportunity has allowed it to thrive. This is evident in the most recent Core Platform Survey conducted by the American Bankers Association – customers rank Jack Henry well ahead of the competition. This has helped support a decades- long trend of market share gains. Shares have de-rated this year as investors have priced in the risk of disruption from AI. The market has largely been undiscerning in its treatment of software businesses, but we think there’s value in forming a more nuanced view. Banking software is heavily regulated, cybersecurity is paramount, and local banks are in the business of serving their local community, not building technology. Given the mispriced AI risk attached to Jack Henry shares, we were able to buy at a valuation discount to the broader market – an unusual entry point for a business of this quality. CDW Corp. (CDW) – Large Cap/Global CDW is a leading multi-brand provider of technology products, services, and solutions, serving over 250,000 commercial, government, education, and healthcare customers across the U.S., UK, and Canada. The company sells through a workforce of roughly 10,900 customer-facing employees, including sellers, highly skilled specialists, and engineers. Scale is a key competitive advantage, as access to more than 100,000 products from over 1,000 vendor brands allows CDW to embed itself across a customer’s IT procurement, integration, financing, and lifecycle management needs in a way that smaller resellers simply can’t match. That breadth, paired with deep vendor certifications, has produced a sticky, relationship-driven franchise with a long history of share gains. Results have been held back in recent years by weak corporate IT spending, and the market has grown increasingly concerned about CDW’s role in delivering AI solutions to customers, pressuring both the share price and valuation. We believe that the air pocket in IT spending is temporary, and that CDW will in fact play a vital role in helping customers implement AI. Beyond that, we see AI as a net positive for demand over time: the buildout of AI-ready infrastructure, the modernization of legacy data environments, and the general increase in IT activity required to support these initiatives should all flow through as incremental opportunity for CDW. The pace of change and complexity across the IT landscape has increased dramatically, and we think this only deepens customer reliance on a trusted third-party expert to help them navigate it – positioning CDW well to capture this demand as customers work through it. The market, however, appears to be taking the opposite view. We see this as a divergence between our outlook and the market’s and believe that gap is precisely what creates an attractive entry point today. If our view plays out, CDW should be able to deliver accelerating EPS growth while being re-rated– a combination that, together with a shareholder-friendly capital return framework, sets up a very attractive investment opportunity.
