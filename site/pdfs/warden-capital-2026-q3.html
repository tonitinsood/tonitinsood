War Cap Q3 Update

May you live in interesting times

Hawkins Entrekin
Oct 16, 2026

Welcome to our quarterly update, but first please read our disclaimer HERE. Nothing in here is investment advice, and we may have positions in any securities mentioned, & may sell them at any time.

This was quite the volatile quarter for us. My returns were 13.31% YTD, down a bit from Q2, vs 9.91% & 12.73% for the USRT and S&P respectively. Since inception its 228.77%, vs 77.24% for the USRT & 187.88% for the S&P.

At one point we were a good bit above Q2 figures, then we unfortunately entered into a big downswing near the end of the quarter. And now as of this writing we are actually up again a good bit, around ~24.5% net returns now YTD. So a fair amount of volatility around the quarter end.1

I will go into the drivers of our performance in a bit, but first I want to talk about the overall market a bit.

The dispersion of potential outcomes continues to remain very high in my view, about as wide as I have ever seen things. The two biggest stories continue to be Hormuz and AI. Things were looking a bit better on Hormuz with increased oil flows transiting the strait, and at one point it even got close to pre-crisis levels. However Iran decided they had enough of that and has been on a blitz of boat strikes in the past 2 weeks. We don’t have the latest tanker data but it will likely be significantly curtailed again, meaning stock draws will pick up steam again. At some point these draws will cause a crisis if there is no resolution. Arguably we are already almost there with diesel prices. The economic impact is hard to quantify, but I can’t imagine it will be good. We are already starting to see the effects in higher inflation, with the Fed deciding to raise rates and signaling they may continue to do so. I am hopeful that some kind of deal is reached, but the risk things spiral is very real here.

The increase in inflation combined with ongoing massive deficits have led to a pretty significant government bond sell off, with the 10 year yield up to ~5.075% as of the writing, up over 100 bps from earlier in 20262. Very painful for anyone who relies on financing.

The deficit issue is a whole can of worms unto itself that I won’t spend any time on here or else this letter will drag on forever. I’m not sure if something will break this cycle but at some point there will be a reckoning with a need to reduce spending or raise taxes.

Whatever the cause, the increase in financing costs has not been friendly to the AI boom given its increasing reliance on debt. The bigger problem though is that recent data from Ramp shows overall token spending has basically flatlined over the last 3 months.

From Ramp’s excellent AI Index, link here. Lots of other interesting data in the report.

Even more timely, Anthropic recently shared Q3 numbers for $16.8B, but notably did not share recent ARR figures. However we can infer roughly where their ARR would be today as the last ARR report was ~$65 billion in July, or ~$16.25b/quarter, which means that revenue did not grow much from July, maybe 5-10% roughly. This roughly gels with the Ramp alt data on token spending.

This is hugely problematic for an industry which needs to have its growth continue to accelerate to even begin to rationalize the capex being spent. I have written about this before HERE, commenting on the similarities between AI and the shale oil business, and the capex investment has grown massively since then along with the corresponding revenues required to rationalize the investment.

What’s interesting is that token usage has continued to grow quickly, but that costs have decreased just as fast, yielding ~zero revenue growth in existing customers.3 Supply has essentially begun to overtake demand. What is scary about this is that supply is set to continue to grow at a huge clip over the next few years as the current wave of data centers is finished with newer and much more powerful chips, with further gains likely coming from improvements to algorithmic efficiency. So to turn the corner and keep the party going AI needs another demand boost like we saw earlier this year from agentic adoption. This may well occur! I frankly did not see the agentic boom coming this year. But I would bet that spending begins to level off from here as the law of large numbers begins to catch up with the industry even as capabilities continue to advance.

Another point for the bear case is that ~50% of chips are used for training, and some meaningful share of inference also appears to be devoted to AI training via distillation, auto-research & RL environment creation by third parties, although this number is unclear I would guess it could be as high as 15-25%. If/when people decide that massive investment in the newest models is not worth it (to go back to my shale analogy, when investors refuse to finance new well drilling), that capacity would be reallocated to inference and thus further increase supply. This could create a vicious cycle where weakness causes demand loss which causes greater supply which in turn begets further weakness. Imagine if 50% of oil demand was somehow related to drilling of new wells - capital heavy businesses like this are already inherently cyclical, and with a huge share of supply consumed by the industry itself this cyclicality would be turbocharged.

The reality is, for the model companies to generate over $1 trillion in revenue/year within the next few years (which is likely required to justify the capex as I have written about previously), we would need to see huge productivity gains from AI throughout the economy and rapid GDP growth, probably 4-5%. This may well be possible, but there is no evidence of it thus far as productivity and GDP growth stats show no beginnings of an uptrend (indeed AI investment is a pretty large component of GDP growth already as it is). I would love to see 4-5% GDP growth but I will take the under here - I think you need proper AGI for that to occur, and progress on that front seems limited4. I do think maybe we get to really strong GDP growth eventually with a robotics boom, but I would guess that is going to be the next cycle, rather than this one.

The market seems to be waking up to the possibility the party may be close to ending, with significant AI weakness the last few weeks which accelerated significantly in the last few days (although it bounced today). Bellwether Coreweave is down nearly 24% from recent highs, and is retracing post Leopold blow up lows.

Even wilder, its 2032 debt which was issued at an already high 9.70% is now trading at ~14-15% or about 80 cents on the dollar.

That is distressed pricing. Its also a big problem for the neocloud ecosystem as the whole thing is reliant upon raising loads of capital from the debt & equity markets. We already saw one IPO get pulled due to lack of demand, an Australian firm called Firmus. If investors lose confidence it could get rough.

If I had to guess though, I would wager that the AI trade isn’t quite fully dead yet, and that recent events are more akin to a 2007 situation, where warning signs begin showing up & some prices start to move, but many bulls remain optimistic. As to when it finally ends I don’t know, but it sure seems like we are entering the end game. AI has become all encompassing and if the market panics this much over meh data I worry what would happen on really bad results. And who knows, perhaps revenue growth reaccelerates and the party can continue, although I have trouble seeing it lasting much more than a year from here as Ant and OAI revenues get so large that they begin to encounter some real constraints on their growth. Going from $120b today to $240b is much different than going from $240b to $480b, which is dramatically different still from $480b to ~$1 trillion, but this is exactly what needs to happen to keep the boom on track.

Portfolio Puts and Takes

The quarter was very good for some of our stocks, and very bad for others, with the effects of these roughly offsetting.

The biggest strengths were our software positions, Gitlab and Atlassian, which were our two largest positions for awhile. They are both up ~100% plus since we bought them earlier in the year, and in my view are much closer to fair value. I have been selling these down and harvesting losses from our short book to try and offset some of the gains, but am waiting for long term capital gains treatment next year before fully selling these.

AI has not in fact killed SaaS, and the market recognized this fact fairly quickly, leading to some incredible returns here.

Another good win was an investment in Bumble, which recently agreed to be taken private at $6.05 / share. We were invested in the common around $2.65 and $5 long term leap calls around $0.3, both of which did very nicely. This happened post quarter end, and is a healthy part of our return to ~24% net as of this writing.

The other part of our portfolio that has done really well since quarter end is our short book, which is quite substantial. In particular we have a good sized long term put position in quantum company QBTS. QBTS earns very little revenue ($3mm last quarter), has very limited prospects of revenue any time soon, and still trades at a $4.5 billion market cap (itself down significantly from highs!). Amazingly you can still buy $5 puts for Jan 2029 for under a dollar, which is kind of crazy in my view. The company has ~$1.3 /share in net cash as of Q3, and is burning ~$0.50 per year. By the time the puts expire the company will be nearly out of cash, and will likely have to do massively dilutive equity raises before expiration. There is zero floor to value here, and in my view the stock is likely to be sub $1 by the expiration of the puts. Quantum may well pan out as a big business at some point, but its highly unclear how large or profitable it will ever be, and most importantly, QBTS doesn’t even do proper quantum computing like Google or IBM does. It does something called ‘quantum annealing’, which only works for very specific optimization problems. Even better, just this year classical computer scientists have shown you don’t even need quantum computing to do this, you can do a better job for much cheaper using new algos on normal computers that simulate quantum effects.5

Usually an attractive short like this has such high implied volatility that even the far out of the money puts are a ~2x return or so (heck Coreweave is a real business with actual cash flow & assets and maxes at ~3x if it goes bankrupt). But amazingly the pricing on these is cheap enough that you can 4x+ fairly reasonably in my view. Talk about an amazing hedge! And the best part is there is a good chance it pays off even if we don’t have a recession - just look at how all the meme stocks did in 2022.

The shorts have been a big net drag on returns over the past 2 years, but I continue to believe they will pay off & offer us a good hedge against market and economic weakness, which I believe is wise given how exuberant the market has been.

And speaking of drags, the biggest weakness this quarter came from Unique, which dropped significantly on a bit less clean than ideal 4 year results6. Much ink has been shed on this elsewhere, but my quick view is that the 4 year data is actually quite good in an absolute sense and in my view removes some of the tail risks on the drug approach not working longer term7. The trial did miss statistical significance on its 4 year target indication of cUHDRS, with 44% slowing but did hit statistical significance on the 4 year TFC with 61% slowing (these are two different ways to measure disease progression). The reason for the miss though was not the patients rate of decline accelerating significantly, but instead the natural history comparator looking much better making the slowing look relatively smaller. This appears to be very likely due to the sickest patients dropping out of the natural history control group as it becomes more difficult to travel to the testing centers and take the tests, which makes the natural control group look better than a typical patient pool. 8

This isn’t just random speculation, we actually have excellent data to support just this conclusion from Roche’s failed Huntington’s trial. Roche made the data available and several scientists wrote a good paper which showed in detail how the patients who dropped out of the trial were progressing 50% faster than those who stayed, which made the end year readings look artificially better than the full patient pool, because the worst patients dropped out.

Link to the paper is here, but what is really interesting are the supplements which you’ll need to download separately.

So I would say my confidence this drug works is pretty high now. However I do not know what the FDA will do & there is definitely some risk they ask for a phase 3 trial, which isn’t the end of the world but would likely hit the stock price. But on the whole I would lean towards FDA granting approval next year as is with a confirmatory trial. This is for several reasons - the 3 year data is good, which is the basis of their submission (and indeed it got better in the recent release with more patients hitting 3 years), I think the FDA will understand this drop out issue, and most importantly, the FDA seems to prefer TFC as a measure of disease progression anyways, as this is what they are going to require the confirmatory trial’s endpoint would be. It would be strange if the FDA decided TFC is the proper end point, but then didn’t approve when the 4 year TFC was very good. But you never know in bioland!

The investment has still been quite good for us as I sold a good bit already earlier in the year, and my plan for now is to hold the remaining Qure until approval be it in 1 year or 4 years9 as I think the risk/reward is quite good at crudely maybe 0.4x on the downside and ~3-4x on the upside if not even higher.

But Qure was not the only weak performer. Bio in general has been really weak, and our two other bio investments also suffered a good bit. Another larger contributor of weakness was a new software position, Intuit. I rotated some of the Team/Gitlab gains into Intuit (the maker of Quickbooks as well as the tax prep software), thinking it offered a better risk/reward… and then it promptly crashed 25% while Gitlab and Team have continued to rally. Cest la vie! The flip side of this is that I think Intuit is very cheap right now, and I have invested more as it fell and it is now our largest position. Intuit is down ~49% in the last year, and was down nearly 65% from its highs as of the end of the quarter. That is just about the biggest drawdown ever, worse even than the 2000 bubble or the GFC.

Intuit is trading very cheaply for a business of its quality - its around a ~16x P/E today on my numbers, and a forward ~14.35x based on management’s guidance for next year. Intuit has guided to high teens earnings growth for the next several years (on low double digit revenue growth), and a whopping 23% EPS growth for next year. Business has been, and continues to be, quite good! The only weakness is the DIY tax prep segment, which looks like its suffering from some competition from people using AI to help file their taxes. This is only ~11% of revenues, so weakness here should easily be more than offset by growth elsewhere.

As to why the stock is so cheap, its impossible to truly know as always but here as my guesses. The first is the tax prep business - this is 25% of revenues overall. If the non DIY segment were also hit hard by AI, that would become a stiffer headwind (although still manageable IMO). However I think if anything Intuit is an AI winner here - most non DIY people are going to want to have a human sign off on their tax returns at the end of the day, and this is what Intuit offers for its higher end tax offerings. While Intuit may lose a little share at the lower end, it also may be able to take share from the traditional tax prep industry at the higher end, as Intuit’s AI powered offerings are now much more competitive with a traditional fully manual tax process. People seem to forget that a huge number of tax returns are still done manually by human accountants today - Intuit estimates it is only ~8% of human assisted take prep by revenue, and that 92% is all opportunity for Intuit. And management is only guiding to 2-3% growth for 2027, so the current growth profile already reflects this becoming a ~GDP grower segment, which to me seems reasonable. AI also obviously could help Intuits margins on its higher end tax prep business as more labor is automated.

The second reason is perhaps fears relating to the durability of the Quickbooks franchise. I think this one is fairly low risk - accounting software to me seems one of the more resistant verticals to AI. Switching costs are very high, and the software cost is low. Its mission critical software so the vibe coding your own risk is also quite low. I think Intuit is again if anything an AI winner here - accounting is a great domain to apply AI to, but the stakes are extremely high and I am willing to bet the vast majority of companies will want to use someone else’s trusted solution to deploy AI in their accounting, and Intuit is by far and away the company best positioned to do that. Which means lots of additional upselling opportunities for Intuit.

Intuit is probably the most ‘Buffett like’ investment I have found, where you can invest in an incredible franchise at a very reasonable going in multiple and likely healthy growth to continue.10

Wrapping Up

All in all, it has been quite an interesting last couple of months and indeed year. And I don’t expect things to slow down anytime soon. My focus remains on attempting to preserve capital in the teeth of what I view as a very frothy market, and I am quite pleased we have managed to have such strong returns this year despite not owning any of the top drivers of the index returns, which is to say mostly the semiconductor complex. The median stock this year is down something like 17% - the bull market has gotten very narrow, which is often associated with future market weakness.

I am hopeful that if/when the AI boom turns to a bust, we will be able to avoid the worst of the damage. And if we can really have an AI powered 4%+ growth boom, well that would be wonderful. But time will tell.
