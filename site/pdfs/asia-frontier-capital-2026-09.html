AFC Uzbekistan Fund - Manager Comment
 

 

The AFC Uzbekistan Fund Class F shares returned +0.9% in September 2026 with a NAV of USD 2,582.37, bringing the year-to-date return to +70.8%. The return since inception (29th March 2019) now stands at +158.2%, representing an annualised return of +13.5% p.a.

September saw the AFC Uzbekistan Fund achieve another all-time high NAV as investor interest in Uzbekistan continues to grow. We have had several dozen investor meetings in Tashkent in recent weeks as investors fly in for tourism, see investment opportunities and vice versa.

The Uzbek Commodity Exchange Launches Derivatives Trading

The Uzbek Commodity Exchange (TSE: URTS) was the first investment we ever made in Uzbekistan back in 2018 (initially through the AFC Asia Frontier Fund, prior to the launch of the AFC Uzbekistan Fund), as it was the publicly listed company with the most obvious leverage to Uzbekistan’s economic growth story when the world was still not paying attention to the vast transformation the country was undergoing. It’s also one of the blue-chips I have written about in the past that every foreign investor who visits Uzbekistan would meet and subsequently wants to own.

At the onset of Uzbekistan’s opening, the government required most government services, including state-owned construction materials companies, to transact through URTS to enhance transparency. This led to a phenomenal growth story that continues to this day, though of course the valuation isn’t as “dirt cheap” as it once was.

As Uzbekistan continues to evolve, so has URTS. On 17th September 2026, the company, alongside its partner, Phillip Nova, part of Singapore’s Phillip Capital Group, launched UZEX Global JV LLC (“UZEX Global”), a joint venture established by the Uzbek Commodity Exchange and Phillip Trading to provide Uzbek traders access to international futures markets, including the CME Group, Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) and the London Metal Exchange (LME), enabling them to hedge their positions in the market. Phase II of the derivatives platform will introduce derivatives trading for local products, which will open the door for new market participants such as construction companies, who will be able to forward purchase cement, for example, whose prices can fluctuate wildly based on the time of the year. This, along with the eventual introduction of electricity and natural gas trading, should add significant revenue growth potential to the company over the coming years and is not priced into the current stock price.

On 21st September 2026, as part of the 5th AFC Uzbekistan Tour, we visited URTS and took the photo below on their trading floor. In recent years, URTS launched digital trading for its clients, so there were a handful of traders at their desks who have the option of where to work.

A Visit to the Tashkent Stock Exchange

With Mr. Fayzulla Tashov having replaced George Paresishvili as CEO of the Tashkent Stock Exchange in late 2025, I figured it would be worth meeting to get an update on plans for further developing capital markets infrastructure as Uzbekistan aims to attract further inbound capital flows. With the local bond market expected to see over USD 100 million in Uzbek Som issuance by year-end and, of course, the successful IPO of UzNIF in May 2026, it is best to strike while the iron is hot.

In so few words, nothing moves fast in Uzbekistan, but my meeting with Mr. Tashov indicates a sea change in initiative if they can execute. He acknowledged that the Tashkent Stock Exchange website has a variety of errors (which they are actively addressing), that they have outgrown the old (but electronic) trading system licensed by the Korean Stock Exchange and which they plan to replace, and that connectivity and information flow to third-party information providers such as FactSet and Bloomberg needs to be improved. To give some context, I introduced the Tashkent Stock Exchange to Bloomberg in 2018, and initial integration of data feeds only started after COVID, after fighting tooth and nail for connectivity, arguing that any foreign institutional investor will first look up Uzbek capital markets information there.

The above-mentioned issues all could have been resolved years ago, but former management teams of the Tashkent Stock Exchange were either blocked or not interested in enacting change. My meeting with Mr. Tashov was frankly refreshing, and President Mirziyoyev has been behind capital markets reforms for years, so hopefully the infrastructure upgrades begin to accelerate.

The exchange has held meetings with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, and there is discussion to establish a connection allowing Uzbeks to trade Hong Kong Equities and for brokers in Hong Kong to have access to Uzbek equities. After integration with Hong Kong, the Tashkent Stock Exchange will explore collaboration with Tabadul (a regional capital markets infrastructure from the Abu Dhabi Stock Exchange that allows brokers in the UAE to access Gulf and Central Asian markets (now the Astana Stock Exchange and Kazakhstan Stock Exchange)). Further, the online trading platform of the Tashkent Stock Exchange will be discontinued by the end of 2027, with an active request for proposal for software providers who can provide an alternative and more robust platform to the exchange. This is all happening as the exchange itself is presently upgrading its internal software to make data feeds to the likes of Bloomberg, FactSet, Reuters, etc. more robust (including equity price, volume, and financial information, as well as bond pricing) and accurate.

Upgrading the infrastructure was great, but the most interesting part of our meeting was how underpenetrated the Uzbek retail market is. Mr. Tashov provided colour on the immense upside Uzbekistan’s market participation offers as he told me there are 1,100,000 brokerage accounts in the country. Roughly 7% of them are active (most are legacy and inactive), so about 77,000, up from about 55,000 two years ago. Year-to-date, another 10,000 brokerage accounts were opened in anticipation of the UzNIF IPO, so a rough 13% increase.

In the grand scheme of things, this is literally nothing! And that’s what’s so attractive. Hopefully these statistics put some credence to my many years of harping in these letters on local investor participation driving the next re-rating. The big question to ask is what will Uzbekistan’s capital markets look like in five to ten years when the middle class is larger, there are more listed corporate bonds and equities, and we get to 500,000 or 1,000,000 active brokerage accounts (which is still next to nothing as the country’s population is expected to hit 52 million by 2050), not accounting for the hopeful Hong Kong and Tabadul connections? While no country’s development is linear, the secular upward trajectory looks well intact and what continues to unfold remains exciting.

 AFC Asia Frontier Fund - Manager Comment

 

The AFC Asia Frontier Fund (AAFF) USD A-shares returned −1.8% in September 2026 with a NAV of USD 2,500.68. The MSCI Frontier Markets Asia Net Total Return USD Index lost 3.6%, while the MSCI Frontier Markets Net Total Return USD Index lost 4.3%, and the MSCI World Net Total Return USD Index lost 1.2%. Year to date, the fund returned +9.2% while the MSCI Frontier Markets Asia Net Total Return USD Index returned +0.9% during the same period. The performance of the AFC Asia Frontier Fund USD A-shares since inception on 30th March 2012 now stands at +150.1%, while the MSCI Frontier Markets Asia Net Total Return USD Index increased +25.7% during the same period. The fund’s annualised performance over 5 years is +9.4% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.50 and a Sortino ratio of 0.70. The broad diversification of the fund’s portfolio has resulted in low risk with an annualised volatility of 10.6% and a correlation of the fund versus the MSCI World Net Total Return USD Index of 0.51, all based on monthly observations since inception.

After a strong performance by the fund in August, geopolitical uncertainties in the Middle East heightened again, leading to higher crude oil prices. This hurt investor sentiment in many Asian frontier stock markets like Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, and Vietnam, which are all dependent on imported crude oil. 

However, with the Middle East conflict now entering its eighth month, the countries in our universe which are dependent on energy imports have managed their overall macroeconomic position in a resilient manner precisely because of the fact that they have significantly strengthened their fiscal and external account buffers. This is the reason why their stock markets are not seeing any major panic and their currencies have been broadly stable.

A key measure of the resilience of our Asian frontier economies is the continuing sovereign credit rating upgrades coming through this year despite the ongoing Middle East conflict. This again is a reflection of the much stronger fiscal and external account buffers that our country universe has built up compared to 2022, when the conflict in Ukraine broke out.

After Kazakhstan, Pakistan, and Uzbekistan received sovereign credit upgrades this year, Sri Lanka’s sovereign credit rating was upgraded by Fitch Ratings to ‘B-’ from ‘CCC+’. Sri Lanka has shown a remarkable macroeconomic recovery since its credit default in April 2022. GDP growth has averaged around 5% over the last two years, tourist arrivals are seeing a major uptick in momentum, and more importantly, this has been backed by solid political stability since the parliamentary elections of November 2024.

The economic numbers are now showing the advantage of this geography – in 2Q26, Oman’s GDP growth was +5.2% YoY, which is significantly better than all of its neighbouring countries, mainly because it can still export its crude oil and other commodities without any hindrance.

AFC Vietnam Fund - Manager Comment

 

The AFC Vietnam Fund returned −3.7% in September with a NAV of USD 3,111.71, bringing the 2026 return to −12.4% and the return since inception to +211.2%. This month, the fund underperformed the benchmark, the Ho Chi Minh City VN Index, which lost 3.1% in USD terms. The fund’s annualised return since inception stands at +9.3% p.a. The broad diversification of the fund’s portfolio resulted in an annualised volatility of 14.83%, a Sharpe ratio of 0.49, and a low correlation of the fund versus the MSCI World Index USD of 0.48, all based on monthly observations since inception.

September was a particularly challenging month for global financial markets. Renewed escalation in the conflict between the United States and Iran pushed oil prices sharply higher, with Brent crude briefly rising above USD 100 per barrel for the first time in six weeks. The escalation and disruptions to Middle Eastern energy infrastructure renewed concerns over global energy supplies, inflation and economic growth. 

The renewed pressure on Vietnamese equities reflected the broader global risk-off environment, as concerns over higher energy prices and the potential impact on inflation and interest rates outweighed the improving domestic fundamentals.
 

VN-Index / VN Small Cap Total Return Index / Vingroup from September 2025 to September 2026

(Source: Bloomberg)
 

Looking Beneath the Surface

The chart above illustrates a feature of the Vietnamese market that is easy to miss when looking only at the headline VN-Index. Since 29th September 2025, the VN-Index has gained just 6%, while Vingroup (VIC) has risen by approximately 161%. Given VIC's outsized index weighting, this single stock has had a disproportionate impact on the headline number — masking much weaker performance across the rest of the market, where many companies with strong earnings growth have nonetheless seen their share prices decline.

The divergence becomes even clearer once VIC is stripped out. Over the same period, the VN Small Cap Total Return Index (VNSCTR) has actually fallen by around 20%, even as the headline VN-Index posted a modest gain. In other words, small- and mid-cap Vietnamese equities, the segment of the market where the AFC Vietnam Fund is predominantly invested, have been in outright decline for a full year, a picture entirely obscured by the index-level performance.

VIC's valuation also carries an unusual feature rooted in market perception rather than fundamentals. It is widely regarded as one of the most politically influenced stocks in Vietnam, and despite being the largest company on the exchange by both market cap and index weight, Bloomberg currently shows only two active analysts are rating this stock, versus the 15–20 analysts typically covering companies of comparable size. This scarcity of coverage underscores how difficult and politically exposed VIC is to assess through conventional fundamental analysis.

Together, these facts explain why the VN-Index can give a misleading impression of the broader market: a rising index does not necessarily mean Vietnamese equities as a whole are performing well. Beneath the headline number, small- and mid-cap companies outside the Vingroup ecosystem have seen meaningful share-price declines despite continued earnings growth. For a fundamental, bottom-up investor like the AFC Vietnam Fund, this distinction matters: the performance of one heavily weighted index component can obscure both the severity of the small-cap drawdown and the increasingly attractive valuations building up across the broader market.

Market Developments

At the same time, global monetary conditions became less supportive. On 16th September 2026, the U.S. Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00%, its first rate increase since July 2023. The decision reflected the Fed’s assessment that inflation remained elevated, while economic activity continued to expand at a solid pace.

The combination of higher energy prices, renewed inflation concerns and tighter monetary policy pushed government bond yields higher and created a more challenging environment for global equities. Together, these factors reinforced the broader risk-off sentiment that weighed on markets during September.

Vietnam Enters the Emerging Market Universe

September marked a historic milestone for Vietnam’s capital market as the country officially entered FTSE Russell’s Secondary Emerging Market universe. Vietnam became the 24th market represented in the FTSE Emerging Index and the 49th country represented in the FTSE All-World Index. The inclusion will be implemented in four stages through September 2027, with FTSE estimating that the transition could ultimately attract up to USD 6 bn of capital into Vietnamese equities.

More importantly, the reclassification reflects years of structural reforms aimed at improving the accessibility and infrastructure of Vietnam’s capital market. FTSE Russell highlighted several important improvements, including the removal of pre-funding requirements for foreign institutional investors, the introduction of a non-prefunding model, improved procedures for failed trades and better access through global brokers. These changes enabled Vietnam to meet the requirements for Secondary Emerging Market status.

Vietnamese equities are now eligible for inclusion in major global benchmarks, including the FTSE Emerging, FTSE Global All Cap and FTSE All-World indices, significantly increasing the market’s visibility among international institutional investors. While the VN-Index declined amid September’s challenging global environment, the reclassification represents a meaningful structural improvement that should support broader foreign participation, deeper market liquidity, and Vietnam’s continued integration into global capital markets.

Strong Growth, Attractive Valuations

Despite the difficult global environment, the Vietnamese equity market continues to offer an attractive combination of earnings growth and reasonable valuations. The market currently trades at approximately 12.7x 2026 forward earnings, while consensus estimates point to 2026 EPS growth of around 15% and an ROE of approximately 15%.

For the AFC Vietnam Fund, the valuation picture is considerably more attractive. The portfolio currently trades at a trailing P/E of just 8.2x and a 2026 forward P/E of only 6.5x, representing a substantial discount to the broader Vietnamese market.

At 6.5x forward earnings, the portfolio has an earnings yield of approximately 15.4%. In other words, the companies in the portfolio are expected to generate earnings equivalent to roughly 15.4% of their current market value over the coming year. This is particularly noteworthy given that many of our portfolio companies continue to deliver solid earnings growth.

In our view, the combination of strong earnings growth, high returns on equity, and exceptionally low valuations suggests that share prices do not yet fully reflect the underlying fundamentals of many of our portfolio companies or Vietnam’s longer-term economic growth potential.

FDI Continues to Accelerate

Foreign direct investment (FDI) remains another important pillar of Vietnam’s growth story. Total registered FDI reached USD 40.6 bn in the first eight months of 2026, a new record high and an increase of 55.4% year-on-year, while disbursed FDI rose 12% to USD 17.3 bn.

The importance of foreign investment is also evident in Vietnam’s trade structure. Foreign-invested companies generated USD 300.4 bn of exports during the first eight months, up 26.9% year-on-year and accounting for more than 80% of Vietnam’s total exports. This highlights the increasingly deep integration of Vietnam’s manufacturing sector into global supply chains.

The economic benefits extend well beyond the initial investment. New manufacturing capacity creates demand across industrial parks, construction, logistics, banking, utilities and domestic suppliers, while supporting employment and household incomes. Together with strong export growth and accelerating public investment, with State-budget investment disbursement rising 18.5% year-on-year in the first eight months, continued FDI inflows provide a strong foundation for Vietnam’s medium-term economic growth.

Vietnam-U.S. Trade and Investment Continue to Strengthen

Despite the challenging global environment, Vietnam’s trade relationship with the United States continues to strengthen. Total exports reached USD 374.8 bn in the first eight months of 2026, a new all-time high and an increase of 22.4% year-on-year. The United States remained Vietnam’s largest export market, with exports reaching approximately USD 122 bn, up 23.2%, despite ongoing concerns surrounding tariffs, rules of origin, and transshipment.

Economic ties are also expanding beyond traditional trade. Starlink, SpaceX’s satellite internet service, officially launched in Vietnam in August, with residential plans starting at approximately VND 1.13 m per month. Tesla has established a local entity, Tesla Motors Vietnam LLC, while Amazon has received regulatory approval for a private satellite network. Meanwhile, Wistron, a major electronics manufacturer and supplier to the global technology sector, continues to expand its presence in Vietnam.

Taken together, these developments highlight the increasingly broad nature of Vietnam-U.S. economic relations. The relationship is no longer limited to trade in manufactured goods, but is increasingly extending into technology, telecommunications, advanced manufacturing and digital infrastructure. This further reinforces Vietnam’s position as an increasingly important part of global supply chains and a growing destination for U.S.-linked investment.

The Iraq Stock Exchange Goes Live on the Abu Dhabi Stock Exchange’s Tabadul Platform

In a positive development for the Iraqi equity market, the Iraq Stock Exchange (ISX), on 29th September 2026, became the eighth market to go live on the Tabadul platform, having signed the integration agreement on 22nd April 2025. Tabadul is the Abu Dhabi Stock Exchange (ADX)’s digital exchange network for a mutual market-access that essentially allows investors on a connected member exchange to trade directly on the ADX, and vice versa, through their local brokers, with settlement in local currencies. Membership therefore offers more than trading access; it is designed to remove the complexities of registering with each exchange and settling trades. Each exchange, its depository and its qualified brokers must resolve these issues before a link goes live in both directions, hence the lag between signing and going live, and why only eight of Tabadul’s eleven member exchanges are live so far. In Iraq’s case, the link that went live on 29th September gives ISX investors access to ADX-listed securities; however, trade-settlement details are yet to be ironed out, so trading access is effectively conditional on resolving these issues. Access to the ISX for ADX-side investors is expected to follow once trade-settlement issues on the ISX’s side are resolved.

Trading values on Tabadul have grown since its launch in 2022; however, the platform does not yet have enough trading history across different market cycles to allow a full assessment of its benefits to connected exchanges. Nevertheless, for the Iraqi equity market, two-way connectivity – opening the ISX to a far larger investor base without the complexities of cross-border settlement – could be transformative for the ISX’s trading volumes, which are dwarfed by those of the ADX. In turn, this could provide a significant boost to ISX equity prices, should ADX investors seek exposure to Iraq’s economic transformation. The ADX’s investor base includes, in addition to the sizeable UAE-based investor community, many of the region’s retail and high-net-worth investors, and many international institutional investors, especially those focused on emerging and frontier markets.

Over the last two months, the market’s decline took the RSISX USD Index towards the low end of its multi-year uptrend, thereby testing the assertion made in our monthly updates over the past two years that, from a technical analysis perspective, it continues to consolidate its gains within this uptrend. The two-month lag between oil exports and the receipt of their revenues means that the full benefit of revenues from the August–September export recovery will not be felt until October–November (chart below) – in much the same way that the revenue shock from the Strait of Hormuz’s closure was not felt immediately. Non-oil revenues will continue to cushion, but cannot fully offset, the year-on-year drop in oil revenues; thus, the year’s projected budget deficit will be much larger than in recent years. The deficit will continue to be contained by tight cost controls, including the build-up of arrears, and funded by the issuance of domestic debt in the form of treasury bills (T-bills). In time, the meaningfully increased T-bill issuance will play a major role in developing the country’s bond market. This should ultimately bring “bond market discipline”, with the potential to correct the structural imbalances between current and investment spending that have been perpetuated in every budget over the last two decades.
